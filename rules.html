Bid EuchreOfficial Rules

4 Players · Partnership · High & Low · Trick-Taking

I Overview

Bid Euchre is a partnership trick-taking game blending Euchre-style trump rules with competitive auction bidding. Players bid contracts specifying the number of tricks they'll win, a trump suit (or No Trump), and whether they're playing High or Low. The team that wins the bid then tries to fulfill — or exceed — their contract, while the defenders try to stop them.

Players4 (2 teams of 2; partners sit opposite)
Deck2 standard Euchre decks, J–A only (32 cards)
Cards per Player8 (all cards are dealt)
Game LengthEach player deals twice, or first to 42 points

II The Deck

Combine two standard Euchre decks and remove all cards except Jacks, Queens, Kings, and Aces. This yields 32 cards — 8 per suit, with two identical copies of every card. This duplication creates a special rule throughout the game: when two identical cards are played in the same trick, the one played first outranks the second.

III Card Ranking

```

High Trump (standard play)

When a trump suit is named and High is called, cards rank from highest to lowest:

#CardNotes
1Right BowerJack of the trump suit
2Left BowerJack of the other suit of the same color as trump
3Ace of trump
4King of trump
5Queen of trump
6Second Ace of trumpDuplicate; loses to the first Ace played
7–8Second King, Second QueenSimilarly outranked by their earlier duplicates
Example — Hearts are trump: J♥ (Right) > J♦ (Left) > A♥ > K♥ > Q♥ > remaining Hearts in order, first played beats second.

Low Trump (reversed)

When Low is called with a trump suit, the ranking completely reverses — the lowest trump wins tricks, not the highest:

#CardNotes
1Queen of trumpLowest — wins in Low
2King of trump
3Ace of trump
4Left BowerJack of same-color suit
5Right BowerJack of trump suit — highest, weakest in Low

Duplicates still follow the first-played rule: the first Queen of trump beats the second.

No Trump High

No trump suit exists. Within each suit, Aces rank highest: A > K > Q > J. Highest card of the led suit wins. First-played wins duplicates.

No Trump Low

No trump suit exists. Within each suit, Jacks rank lowest: J > Q > K > A (reading lowest to highest). The lowest card of the led suit wins the trick. First-played wins duplicates.

```

IV The Deal

The dealer shuffles both decks together and deals all 32 cards clockwise, one at a time, until each player holds 8 cards. After each hand, the deal passes clockwise to the next player. A full game consists of each player dealing twice (8 hands total).

V Bidding

Bidding starts with the player to the dealer''s left and proceeds clockwise. Each player has exactly one opportunity to either pass or make a higher bid than any previous bid. The player who makes the highest bid wins the contract and leads the first trick.

```

Anatomy of a Bid

Every valid bid states three things:

  1. Number of tricks — how many tricks the bidding team pledges to win (3–8)
  2. Trump designation — one of the four suits, or No Trump
  3. High or Low — which card wins each trick
4 Clubs High 5 No Trump Low 6 Hearts High 8 Spades Low

Raising the Bid

Each new bid must name more tricks than the current high bid. Suits carry no ranking — you cannot overcall with the same number of tricks in a different suit or mode. The minimum opening bid is 3 tricks.

Example: If the current bid is "4 Hearts High," the next bidder must bid 5 or more tricks. Bids of "4 Spades" or "4 No Trump" are invalid.
5 Clubs Low ✓ 5 No Trump High ✓ 4 Spades High 4 No Trump Low

Lone Hand Bids

Any bid may be declared Alone, meaning the bidder''s partner sits out the hand with cards face down. A lone bid takes precedence over an equal-trick partnership bid from an opponent — but opponents may counter with their own lone bid at the same or higher trick count.

Partner Exchange (Lone Bids Only)

A lone bidder may optionally exchange cards with their sitting-out partner before play begins:

  • 1-card exchange: Bidder discards 1 card face down; partner passes 1 card face down.
  • 2-card exchange: Same process with 2 cards each.
Critical: Both the bidder and partner must choose and commit to their exchange cards simultaneously, before either sees what the other has passed.
```
✦ ✦ ✦

VI Playing the Hand

```

Opening Lead

The winning bidder leads the first trick. In a Lone hand, the bidder leads even though only three hands are active.

Following Suit

Each player must follow the led suit if able. If a player has no cards of the led suit, they may play any card — including trump. The Left Bower counts as a member of the trump suit, not its face suit, for both leading and following.

Winning a Trick

The winning card is determined by the game mode:

ModeWins with trump playedWins without trump played
HighHighest trump playedHighest card of led suit
LowLowest trump playedLowest card of led suit

The winner of each trick leads the next. Play continues until all 8 tricks are complete.

```

VII Scoring

```

Bidding Team

If the bidding team wins at least as many tricks as they bid, they score the number of tricks actually won (not just the bid amount). If they fall short, they lose points equal to their bid.

BidTricks WonResult
56+6
55+5
54−5

Defending Team

The non-bidding team scores 1 point for every trick they win, regardless of whether the bidding team succeeds or fails.

Lone Hand Scoring

ContractOutcomeScore
Any lone bidSuccessTricks won +1 bonus
8-trick lone (Shoot the Moon)SuccessDouble tricks won (16)
Any lone bidFailureLose full bid amount

Shooting the Moon

A bid of all 8 tricks — "Shoot the Moon" — is the highest possible contract. If successful alone, the bidder''s team scores 16 points. If the contract fails, the team loses 8 points. Defenders still score any tricks they manage to win.

Reneging

A renege occurs when a player fails to follow the led suit despite holding a card of that suit. Agree on the penalty before play begins. Common options:

  • Automatic set: the offending team loses their bid (or the hand is conceded to the defenders)
  • Deduct 3 points from the offending team
  • Replay the hand
House rule: Agree on reneging penalties before the first hand is dealt.
```
✦ ✦ ✦

VIII Winning the Game

The game ends when each player has dealt twice (8 hands), or when a team first reaches the agreed target score (commonly 42 points). The team with the higher score at game end wins. In case of a tie, play one additional hand as a tiebreaker.
